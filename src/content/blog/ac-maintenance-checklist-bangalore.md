Bangalore's climate is unusual for AC maintenance — hot, dusty summers followed by heavy monsoon rain put different kinds of stress on a unit within the same year. A little upkeep in between goes a long way toward avoiding a breakdown on the hottest day of the year.

## Monthly, during heavy use

- **Clean the air filter.** This is the single highest-impact thing you can do yourself — a dirty filter is the most common cause of "weak cooling" complaints.
- **Check the outdoor unit's clearance.** Dust, leaves, and even nearby plants can restrict airflow around the condenser and reduce cooling efficiency.
- **Listen for new noises.** Rattling, buzzing, or grinding sounds that weren't there before are usually easier (and cheaper) to fix early.

## Before summer (February–March)

- Book a **general service** — cleaning the coils, checking refrigerant pressure, and clearing the drain line — before the peak heat months when technicians get booked out fastest.
- Check the drain pipe isn't clogged; a blocked drain is one of the top causes of water leaking from the indoor unit once the AC starts running for longer hours.
- If the unit is more than 8–10 years old, ask a technician whether it's still running efficiently compared to a newer inverter model — older units can cost significantly more to run.

## Before monsoon (May–June)

- **Protect the outdoor unit from direct, heavy rain** where possible, without blocking ventilation — a simple angled cover over the top is usually enough.
- **Recheck the drain pipe.** Combined with humidity, a partially blocked drain during monsoon is a common cause of water stains and leaks indoors.
- Avoid running the AC directly after a power cut or voltage fluctuation, common during Bangalore storms — wait a few minutes for the compressor's internal pressure to stabilize.

## Signs it's time to call a technician, not wait for the next checklist

- Cooling has noticeably weakened over the past few weeks
- Water is leaking from the indoor unit
- The outdoor unit is unusually loud or vibrating
- Electricity bills have crept up without a change in usage

Routine upkeep catches most problems before they become expensive ones — but when something's already wrong, a proper diagnosis beats guesswork.
