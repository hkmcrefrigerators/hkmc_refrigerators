Nothing ruins a Bangalore afternoon faster than an AC that's running but not actually cooling the room. Before you assume the worst, here are the seven most common causes we see on service visits — roughly in the order our technicians check them.

## 1. A dirty or clogged air filter

A blocked filter restricts airflow over the cooling coil, so the unit runs but barely cools. This is the single most common cause of "weak cooling" complaints, and it's the easiest to fix — filters should be cleaned every 2–4 weeks during heavy use.

## 2. Low refrigerant (gas leak)

If your AC blows air that's only slightly cool, or you notice ice forming on the indoor unit, it likely has a refrigerant leak. Refrigerant doesn't get "used up" in normal operation — a low level almost always means a leak that needs to be found and sealed, not just topped up.

## 3. Dirty outdoor unit (condenser)

The outdoor unit needs to reject heat into the surrounding air. If it's caked in dust, covered by plants, or boxed in without clearance, it can't do that efficiently and cooling suffers — especially in dusty Bangalore neighborhoods near construction or main roads.

## 4. Frozen evaporator coil

A layer of ice on the indoor coil usually points to restricted airflow (filter, blocked vents) or low refrigerant. Running the AC in this state can damage the compressor, so switch it off and let it thaw before calling for service.

## 5. Thermostat or sensor issues

Sometimes the AC itself is fine but the temperature sensor is misreading the room, causing the compressor to cycle off too early. This is common in older units and shows up as inconsistent cooling through the day.

## 6. Undersized or overworked unit

A 1-ton AC in a room that really needs 1.5 tons will run constantly and still struggle on the hottest days. If cooling has always felt marginal — not just recently — sizing may be the real issue.

## 7. Compressor wear

On older units, a weakening compressor can't build enough pressure to cool properly. This usually comes with unusual noise or higher-than-normal power bills alongside the weak cooling.

## Quick checks before you book a repair

- Clean or replace the air filter
- Make sure the outdoor unit has at least 2 feet of clearance on all sides
- Check the remote/thermostat is set to cool, not fan-only
- Check the circuit breaker hasn't tripped

If cooling is still weak after these checks, it's time for a proper diagnosis — some of these issues (gas leaks, compressor faults, sensor problems) need the right tools and a trained eye to fix safely.
